## Extracted operating model

1. Purpose & Scope
This plan converts the approved 2026-27 Australian Climate Operational Resilience Outlook into practical divisional preparedness and response actions. Its purpose is to protect people, determine whether severe weather may affect reasonable use of Business Centres, communicate early enough for people to act, and escalate material impacts into the appropriate enterprise arrangements.
It does not replace enterprise emergency management, Property, Technology, workplace safety, business continuity or crisis-management procedures.
Core principle: Do not wait for disruption to occur before communicating or taking proportionate protective action where credible risk to people or Business Centre access is emerging.
2. Operating Model
FORECAST / WARNING  →  EXPOSURE  →  T-LEVEL  →  ACTION  →  COMMUNICATE  →  ESCALATE  →  RECOVER
The plan is designed so a person covering the function can determine within minutes: what authoritative advice exists; who or what is exposed; the current T-level; what action or communication is required; who has authority; and whether enterprise escalation is necessary.

Business Continuity Director | Primary escalation and delegate. Confirm or override posture; approve material T3 safety/attendance actions where required; provide after-hours decision support; escalate material matters to the Divisional Executive.
Divisional Executive | Executive authority for material divisional decisions, prolonged disruption, major operational impacts or matters requiring enterprise engagement. Current role holder and delegate(s) are maintained in Annex A.
People Leaders | Implement divisional direction; support impacted people; identify local impacts; escalate welfare and operational issues.
Enterprise functions | Retain existing authority for building status/closure, enterprise incident/crisis response, Technology, Safety/Security and enterprise-wide communications.

After-hours decision chain: BC&R Lead → BC Director → Divisional Executive / nominated delegate → relevant enterprise escalation arrangement. Contact reachability remains to be confirmed.
Safety safeguard: Where immediate safety is at risk, inability to reach the next escalation level must not delay communication of authoritative emergency advice or reasonable precautionary guidance.
Named primary authorities are confirmed. Divisional Executive delegate(s) and after-hours reachability/contact details remain to be confirmed.

5. Monitoring & Authoritative Sources
Input | Use
Bureau of Meteorology | Forecasts, heat, severe weather, rainfall/flood and tropical cyclone information.
State/Territory emergency and fire agencies | Local warnings, emergency advice, fire danger and community action guidance.
ANZ enterprise advice | Property/site status, enterprise communications, Safety/Security, Technology and incident/crisis arrangements.
Divisional information | People concentration, expected attendance, critical activity, local manager feedback and known operational impacts.

The division should not reinterpret or downgrade official emergency advice. Where official advice directs people to avoid an area, evacuate or take protective action, that advice takes precedence.
Each monthly refresh of the Climate Outlook triggers a baseline check of this plan to determine whether exposure assumptions, thresholds or seasonal readiness need adjustment.
6. T0-T4 Communication & Action Framework
T-levels are condition- and exposure-based. Timing is contextual guidance only, not a threshold criterion. Fast-onset hazards may move directly from T0/T1 to T3; levels may be skipped whenever conditions warrant.
Level | State | Trigger / situation | Required divisional action
T0 | MONITOR | Seasonal/general risk only; no credible near-term material divisional exposure. | Routine monitoring; maintain register and readiness.
T1 | HEADS-UP | Credible emerging threat may affect a Business Centre, commuting area or meaningful workforce concentration. | Validate exposure; alert relevant leaders; check enterprise advice; prepare/issue targeted awareness message where useful.
T2 | PREPARE | Material exposure increasingly likely, with reasonable potential to affect travel, attendance, safety or ability to work. | Confirm affected population/centre; prepare flexible-work arrangements; confirm escalation; increase monitoring; communicate preparation and next update.
T3 | ACT | Official warning, imminent/occurring material disruption, unsafe travel/access, or conditions requiring protective action. | Give clear protective direction within divisional authority; support remote work where possible; escalate site/enterprise issues; track impacts.
T4 | RECOVER | Immediate threat passed but people, access, utilities or operations remain affected. | Welfare/impact check; confirm enterprise advice; manage residual constraints; communicate return-to-normal arrangements.

7. Threshold Decision Test
Question | Assessment
1. What is the authoritative advice? | Forecast, Watch, Warning, Emergency Warning, fire danger rating or local emergency advice.
2. Who/what is exposed? | People concentration, Business Centre, major commuting catchment or travelling team.
3. When could impact occur? | Lead time and rate of change; do not let timing override current conditions.
4. What could it reasonably disrupt? | Safety, travel, centre access, power/connectivity, attendance or critical divisional activity.
5. What action is proportionate now? | Monitor, Heads-up, Prepare, Act, Recover and/or escalate.
6. Could a Critical Operation be affected? | Could the event affect a CPS 230 Critical Operation, supporting resource/dependency, or ability to remain within an applicable impact tolerance? If YES or UNCERTAIN, escalate through enterprise arrangements.

8. Hazard Trigger Matrix
These prompts support consistent assessment; they do not replace official warnings. T1 = Heads-up, T2 = Prepare, T3 = Act.
Hazard | T1 - HEADS-UP | T2 - PREPARE | T3 - ACT
Bushfire / smoke | Credible fire/smoke threat intersects relevant geography. | Worsening conditions likely to affect travel, attendance or centre use. | Emergency/evacuation advice, unsafe access, or fire/smoke materially affecting people or centre use.
Extreme heat | Unusually severe/prolonged heat forecast for relevant workforce/centre. | Credible transport, power or health/safety stress likely to affect attendance/use. | Conditions or official/enterprise advice make travel/centre use unsafe or materially impractical; significant disruption occurring.
Flood / intense rainfall | Flood Watch/significant rainfall forecast intersects relevant geography. | Credible access, transport or local flooding impacts increasingly likely. | Flood Warning/emergency advice; roads, transport, centre access or workforce areas materially affected.
Severe storm | Severe weather/thunderstorm risk intersects relevant geography. | High-confidence forecast with credible travel, power or access impacts. | Dangerous conditions imminent/occurring; material transport, power, property or access impacts.
Tropical severe weather | Tropical system risk intersects relevant workforce/centre geography. | Watch/credible forecast with material travel, access or power implications. | Cyclone Warning/emergency advice or material disruption expected/occurring.
Power / telecommunications | External hazard creates credible utility-disruption risk. | Meaningful outage increasingly likely across exposed workforce/centre. | Material outage affects a meaningful workforce concentration or ability to operate.

10. Communication Standard & Channels
Every severe-weather communication should state: what is happening; where/when it matters; what it means for people/Business Centres; what people should do now; and when the next update will be provided.
Channel rule: Core decision-makers coordinate through Microsoft Teams. Workforce communications are issued by email, supported by People Leader cascade where required.
Escalation does not prevent protective communication. Avoid vague 'we are monitoring' language where a specific action or decision has already been made.
Audience / purpose | Primary channel | Fallback
Impacted workforce - awareness/action | Email | People Leader cascade
People leaders | Email | Microsoft Teams
Core decision-makers | Microsoft Teams | Direct contact / agreed after-hours mechanism
Senior divisional leadership | Email / direct briefing | Microsoft Teams
Enterprise escalation | Refer to BC Manual | Established enterprise escalation process
Urgent local action | Email plus People Leader cascade where practicable | Authoritative enterprise emergency channel where applicable

11. Enterprise & CPS 230 Escalation
This plan does not reproduce ANZ's Critical Operations or impact-tolerance framework. At T2/T3, and whenever material operational disruption is identified, ask whether the event could affect a CPS 230 Critical Operation, supporting resource/dependency, or ability to remain within an applicable impact tolerance.
If YES or UNCERTAIN, escalate through the relevant enterprise Operational Resilience / Business Continuity / incident arrangements. This is a hand-off trigger, not a divisional assessment of enterprise residual risk.

12. Recovery & Return to Normal
Return to normal is based on current conditions and enterprise advice, not simply expiry of a warning. Residual flooding, transport disruption, smoke, utility outages or community impacts may justify continued flexible arrangements.
Recovery check | Question
People | Are affected people safe and reasonably able to work/travel?
Business Centre | Is enterprise advice that the centre is available, and is access reasonably practical?
Utilities / connectivity | Are material outages still affecting the workforce?
Operational activity | Are any critical activities still disrupted or backlogged?
Communication | Have people been told what changed and what arrangements now apply?
Lessons | Does the event require an update to thresholds, contacts, exposure data or this plan?

16. Tabletop Exercise & Approval
The plan remains a draft until the remaining activation gates are complete and this embedded tabletop has been run. The Business Continuity Director is the plan approver; the current role holder is maintained in Annex A. Material tabletop actions must be closed or explicitly accepted before promotion to v1.0.
Exercise objective: demonstrate that the after-hours authority spine, communication model, attendance decision rights, enterprise hand-offs and Live Event Decision Card work under a fast-moving T1→T3 event.
16.1 Scenario
Run outside normal business hours. A fast-moving severe-weather event initially reaches T1 for a location with a meaningful divisional workforce concentration. Conditions deteriorate rapidly to T3. The primary decision-maker is temporarily unavailable; Business Centre/Property status is uncertain; travel and attendance may be unsafe; and there is uncertainty about whether a Critical Operation or supporting dependency could be affected.
16.2 Participants
Required: Business Continuity & Resilience Lead, Business Continuity Director, Divisional Executive or nominated delegate, and representative People Leader(s). Current role holders are maintained in Annex A. Enterprise functions may be simulated using the escalation pathways in the current BC Manual.
16.3 Exercise Injects
Inject 1 - T1: credible warning emerges after hours. Identify exposure, set posture and decide whether an email Heads-up is required.
Inject 2 - Decision-maker unavailable: test escalation from the Business Continuity & Resilience Lead to the Business Continuity Director and onward if required, using Microsoft Teams for core decision coordination.
Inject 3 - T2: forecast confidence increases; planned attendance and commuting are likely to be affected. People Leaders determine flexible-work arrangements within normal authority.
Inject 4 - T3: authoritative advice/conditions indicate travel or attendance may be unsafe. Test Business Continuity Director approval of a broader protective attendance direction and issue workforce communication by email.
Inject 5 - Enterprise uncertainty: Property/site status is unclear and a Critical Operation/supporting dependency may be affected. Use the BC Manual to identify the correct enterprise escalation pathway.
Inject 6 - Recovery: warning passes but transport/utilities remain disrupted. Decide whether normal attendance should resume and set the next communication.
16.4 Evidence to Capture
Record: time of each inject; T-level decision; decision-maker; advice/evidence used; attendance/flexible-work decision; communications issued; enterprise escalation pathway used; next-review time; and any issue encountered using the Live Event Decision Card.
16.5 Pass / Acceptance Criteria
The team identifies the exposed people/Business Centre and selects a defensible T-level without relying on elapsed-time bands.
The authority chain continues when the primary decision-maker is unavailable.
People Leader versus Business Continuity Director attendance authority is understood and applied correctly.
Core decision-makers coordinate effectively through Microsoft Teams and workforce direction is communicated by email.
The BC Manual is successfully used to identify the required enterprise escalation pathway.
The CPS 230 / Critical Operations hand-off question is asked at the appropriate point.
The Live Event Decision Card can be completed and used without needing another section of the plan to make the core decision.
Material gaps/actions are recorded with an owner and due date.
